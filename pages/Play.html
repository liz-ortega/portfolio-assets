Most of my work had been digital, so I took a digital fabrication course to learn CAD, 3D scanning, printing and moldmaking. Three pieces on memory, embodiment and perception.

01 · DIORAMA · 3D SCANNING & PRINTING

Columbus Elementary (1910–2021)

A two-sided diorama of the K-6 school in the Oklahoma City neighborhood where I grew up, demolished in 2021. One side shows Columbus alive; the other, abandoned, behind a fence I printed, then bent by hand to look worn.

My 3D-printed brick kept failing, so I labeled one by hand, inspired by neighbors who saved real bricks as keepsakes. The imperfections felt right: memory holds on to fragments, not exact replicas.

02 · MOLDMAKING & CASTING

Lost Brain

One brain, cast again and again in different materials and colors to explore Depersonalization-Derealization Disorder (DPDR): from a soft, fleeting pink to a sparkly blue that asks what happens when thinking starts to feel outsourced to AI.

Getting silicone into the deep crevices took mold release, vaseline, ties and staples. One cast came out upside down and left a void in the white brain, and that accident ended up fitting the concept.

03 · 3D PRINTING · LED · INSTALLATION

Drifting Outside Myself: a third-person view of the brain

A split, 3D-printed brain with two viewing holes, lit inside with colored LEDs over a mirror and surrounded by handwritten first-person questions, like “Do I feel this way?”

Instead of revealing the inside, it makes you peer in and become an observer of a mind from the outside, the way DPDR is often described. I scoped it down from a tent-sized idea to fit two weeks and printed all weekend to make it.

03 · PAINTING · COMMUNITY ART EVENT

a piece of me in our own space.

When our affinity groups’ salas (living rooms) were reopened and relocated, they needed new decoration. As the Cultural Communities & Mentorship Programming Intern, I came up with an art event to connect all of the salas: a way to bond over our cultures and our thoughts, and to make something of our own for our own spaces.

This is my painting of Big Bridges, the building at Pomona College, which I made while hosting and running the event. It’s really special to me: it goes back to our communities, and I got to leave a little piece of myself in a space that matters to me.
